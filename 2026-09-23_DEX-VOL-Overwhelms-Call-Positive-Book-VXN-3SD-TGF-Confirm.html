DEX/SKEW Read

The session opened with a call-dominant standing book (0DTE DEX +2.89B, 83% call composition), consistent with the positioning carried over from the two prior sessions. As the session progressed, dealer exposure shifted decisively negative — 0DTE DEX (OI+Vol combined) moved to −8.97B and continued deepening to −18.17B, with composition flipping to 66% put-dominant. Isolating the components confirmed the standing open-interest book remained net positive (+1.18B) throughout; the negative shift was driven entirely by same-day traded volume (−19.34B), overwhelming an otherwise unchanged call-heavy base.

SKEW held in a narrow band (0.145 → 0.117 → 0.115) through this move — a materially smaller adjustment than the scale of the DEX shift would suggest. This tells us the put/call IV differential didn't move much; it doesn't tell us whether overall IV moved with the flow, since SKEW only captures the relative gap between the two legs. This divergence — DEX moving decisively while SKEW stays comparatively flat — is a pattern worth tracking for what it implies about follow-through: sustained one-sided flow with a comparatively muted SKEW response.

Structural Findings

30,998 — pre-market 98DTE level, confirmed by heavy same-day volume at the point of contact. This level was marked pre-market as a visually large concentration on the 98DTE chart. Live data showed heavy trading volume concentrated at this same strike as price traded into the zone, before reversing sharply into the decline toward the session low — real transacted activity engaging with the pre-marked level, distinct from (and in addition to) its original OI-based identification. Live volume also showed an even heavier concentration at 31,298, consistent with the pre-market note of a large two-sided call/put bar there — the effective ceiling that session likely sat at or beyond 31,173, which was never reached.

Live GEX (OI) major-level migration, intraday. By the early afternoon, live data showed the GEX-based major positive and major negative levels (OI) sitting close together at 30,796.72 and 30,736.72 respectively — both well below the pre-market 98DTE Major/Call Wall (31,173) and the volume-based major levels (31,096.72 / 30,806.72). This is the Wall Decay Rule ("never assume yesterday's dominant wall is today's dominant wall") observed live, within a single session, rather than only session-to-session: the GEX-based structural center of gravity had moved meaningfully lower by the time of this snapshot.

0DTE put concentration, 30,325–30,575, isolated view. A continuous, dense put-side DEX wall spans this range, with the single heaviest strike at 30,470 — carrying more than $1B in notional alone, roughly 12–15% of the day's entire 0DTE DEX total. An iv_bid/iv_ask spread check confirmed a tight, consistent ~1.2-point bid/ask spread at 30,470, supporting it as genuinely liquid structure rather than size on paper alone. A separate, narrow spread anomaly (wide/crossed quotes) was isolated to 30,440–30,450 (NQ ~30,738–30,748) — a data-quality pocket distinct from the broader zone's otherwise tight liquidity.

Technical note — why the spread check matters here. A large DEX or GEX number at a strike only tells you that real notional size exists there; it says nothing about whether that size sits behind a genuinely liquid, tradeable market. Pulling iv_bid and iv_ask together gives the actual bid/ask spread in IV terms at that specific strike — a tight, consistent spread (as at 30,470) confirms the size is backed by real two-sided quoting; a sudden wide or crossed (bid above ask) spread, as isolated at 30,440–30,450, flags a strike where the quote itself may be stale or thin, and any DEX/GEX reading there should be treated with more caution than the surrounding, well-quoted zone.

Cross-expiry size comparison as a standing practice. Checking a level across 0DTE, 1DTE, and 98DTE separately — rather than reading only the aggregate — clarifies which expiry is actually driving a given concentration's significance. The 30,998 level's standout size specifically on the 98DTE chart (rather than being similarly prominent across all three) is a useful example: a level does not need to be large on every expiry to be structurally significant, but knowing which expiry is contributing the size sharpens the read on how durable that level is likely to be.

Total Gamma Flip Test

Price traded down through the 30,688 Total Gamma Flip level intraday, reaching a session low of 30,640 — approximately 48 points below the flip. The session recovered from that low and closed at 30,778, roughly 90 points above the flip level. This was not a sharp, full reversal off the low — the recovery was gradual, mirroring VXN's own partial (not full) bleed-off from its +3σ extreme over the same stretch — but the Total Gamma Flip held into the close regardless.
