03 — Session Mechanism: A Three-Level Call Wall Stack, a Put-Volume Overwhelm at the Nearest Rung, and a Close Below ZG

RTH opened at 30,219 — a mild +0.402% above ZG, no extreme directional bias. Price initially struggled to advance, and the morning session was really a continuation of a pattern already established in London: the middle level of the call wall stack (30,292) had already been tested twice and rejected both times, a genuine live-confirmed defense rather than incidental wicks. The entire RTH morning played out beneath that same level, unable to clear it. Price eventually pushed to a session high of 30,287 — a third effective rejection at that same 30,292 rung, with the top of the stack (30,392) never tested at all.

From that high, price reversed hard. Rather than a shallow pullback, the decline broke straight back down toward the nearest rung of the stack (30,192), continued through the 1SD baseline (30,168), and drove through ZG (30,098) itself to a session low of 30,054 — a clean undercut, not a wick-and-reclaim. Net GEX read roughly balanced through the morning, with no strong directional lean either way — but the afternoon was a different story: sell-side GEX positioning took control and held through the rest of the session, consistent with price never meaningfully reclaiming ZG. The session closed at 30,080 — below ZG, confirming the breakdown held into the close rather than reverting.

Full Session Chart — MNQU26, 15m: complete Aug 17 RTH session against the level stack. Price pushed up into the 30,292 rejection during the morning, reversed through 30,192, the 1SD baseline, and ZG, then closed at 30,080 — below ZG.

Price tested the nearest stack level (30,192) repeatedly through the decline without breaking it cleanly — each attempt rejected. Around 11:30 AM, put-side GEX volume at that same 30,192 strike grew to roughly 5x the size of the call wall itself — notable specifically because 30,192 was a call wall, not a put wall. At the same time, the call wall's own size was decreasing — a two-sided shift, with put-side pressure surging as the call-side structure defending the level eroded simultaneously. Seeing put-side volume overwhelm a call wall this way is a pattern the framework reads as bearish for that level: it signals the call wall is likely to break, rather than defending it the way put-side volume would at an actual put wall. That's exactly what played out — the level broke shortly after, clearing the way for the final leg down to the 30,054 low.

VXN's behavior tracked this shift. Price fought to hold below its own SD baseline (21.24) during the New York AM session but could not sustain the break, reclaiming baseline shortly after. From there VXN pushed higher through the session, testing up toward +1SD/+2SD (21.44–21.63) around midday and spending the New York PM session oscillating near +1SD, closing at 21.51. VXN rising through the afternoon as price broke down and failed to reclaim ZG is the standard inverse relationship (fear building alongside the decline) — distinct from the stable-skew "VXN tracks price down" pattern seen on genuinely orderly sessions, and consistent with the afternoon's sell-side GEX control being a real, sustained shift rather than a temporary flush.
