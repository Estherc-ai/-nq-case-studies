The session opened at 29,417, inside the pre-market gamma-neutral pocket bounded below by the 0DTE Gamma Flip (29,347) and above by 98DTE call-side open interest concentrated near 29,472/29,462. Price declined from the open to test ZG directly, producing an immediate reaction and a bounce back into the 29,472/29,462 zone — the first of what would become a repeated rejection sequence at that level.

By 10:00 AM, 0DTE DEX had flipped from its negative pre-market baseline (−388.88M) to +1.49B, coinciding with the bounce off ZG toward the wall stack. Unlike a mechanical hedge-driven surge, 0DTE SKEW rose alongside the DEX build (0.078 → 0.094 → 0.213 by 10:15 AM), indicating the call-side positioning defending the 29,472/29,462 zone carried genuine conviction rather than forced dealer hedging.

Price pulled back from the wall stack through 10:30 AM (DEX easing to +861M, SKEW 0.187) before a second decline into ZG at 10:45 AM. This second test swept through the level — producing the session low at 29,334 — before reclaiming ZG on a close back above it. 0DTE DEX remained elevated through the sweep (+1.6B at 10:45 AM), confirming the low formed while call-side positioning was still active rather than during a DEX unwind, distinguishing this session from a Call Hedge Exhaustion pattern where the low typically forms only after DEX collapses.

A rally off the 10:45 AM low carried price back to 29,499 by 11:30 AM — the session high — where it was again rejected at the 29,472/29,462 wall stack. Live GEX data through the session showed this zone strengthening from a moderate pre-market concentration into a large, actively defended stack, with additional smaller call walls added intraday around the original two levels. The Put Wall at 29,522 was never tested at any point in the session.

Following the 11:30 AM rejection, price ranged without any further test of ZG, repeatedly probing the 29,472/29,462 stack (11:45 AM, 12:00 PM) without breaking through. 0DTE DEX oscillated between +243M and +2.14B across the entire post-open window without ever sign-flipping back negative or collapsing to near-zero, while SKEW oscillated in a 0.027–0.213 band without a sustained collapse. This combination — sustained one-sided DEX with oscillating, non-collapsing SKEW — produced compression rather than directional resolution: no breakout through the wall stack (unlike a Gamma Squeeze), and no reversal cascade (unlike Call Hedge Exhaustion). The session closed at 29,392, inside the range, below the wall stack and above ZG.

A third confirming leg is provided by 0DTE σ (IV), which declined steadily and near-monotonically all session — from 0.49 pre-market to 0.17 by 11:45 AM/12:00 PM — even while DEX and SKEW were both actively oscillating. The single largest move came immediately at the open: IV was cut more than half, from 0.49 to 0.23, in the very first 10:00 AM print — the sharpest repricing of the session, and the clearest early signal that the elevated overnight IV was not going to be validated by the price action that followed. A genuine breakout or squeeze typically requires implied volatility to hold or expand alongside the move, as directional conviction keeps option buyers paying up as price continues. Steadily falling IV throughout, despite real two-sided conviction visible in DEX and SKEW, indicated the options market was pricing containment rather than resolution — vol sellers were in control for the entire session, consistent with the range that ultimately held. This decay is a stronger and earlier tell than DEX or SKEW alone: elevated pre-market IV following an overnight move that begins decaying steadily once RTH opens, even while DEX/SKEW appear active, signals the market is pricing a contained range rather than a directional break.
