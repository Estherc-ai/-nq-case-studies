03 — Session Mechanism: A Two-Sided Volume Fight, a Live Put-Volume Spike, and a Full Floor Break

At the open, both call-side and put-side volume ran unusually large simultaneously — a genuine two-sided fight rather than one-directional flow, consistent with net GEX itself staying small and balanced through the early morning, offering no clear lean on its own. No single strike carried a large wall on either side — instead, every strike along the way carried a small one, with tight spreads throughout. Rather than acting as a single rejection point, that distributed structure worked more like a brake: each small wall cost time to work through, so the cumulative drag across many strikes slowed the advance without ever producing one clean bounce — a different texture than a single dominant wall would produce, even if the underlying dealer-hedging mechanism is the same. Call-side volume looked slightly heavier through this stretch, as price pushed up off the overnight decline attempting to reclaim some lost ground.

That attempt was met and rejected by a live put-volume spike at 9:43 AM: two huge put-side volume clusters appeared live, stacked at 29,764 and 29,770 — sharp, unmistakable spikes on the live volume chart, not a gradual buildup and not an OI-based wall. That zone landed almost exactly on what became the session high (29,764) — the market rejecting there and refusing to reclaim any more of the overnight drop. This was the clean entry signal: once price rejected from that live volume spike, it could not reverse clearly again, bleeding steadily down through the rest of the session to a low of 29,515 before closing at 29,559.

The session low broke through the entire pre-market floor stack — not just one level, but all of it: ZG (30,067), Total Gamma Flip (29,732), the Put Wall (29,692), the 1-Day Expected Move (29,611), and even the Asian −1SD statistical band (29,585). A session low landing below Asian −1SD specifically is a genuine statistical extreme, not just a nominal decline — every independent floor-side method the framework tracks was breached by the close.

Breaking Total Gamma Flip specifically marks a regime shift, not just a level break. Since that figure reflects dealer positioning aggregated across the whole 98DTE chain rather than just today's 0DTE, dropping below it — on top of already being below the 0DTE ZG — points to something more structural in dealer positioning than a single-day event, a condition that tends to persist rather than resolve cleanly by the close.

Notably, the actual point move was modest relative to how much ground the session covered structurally. Open to session low was only 175.75 points (29,690.75 → 29,515) — yet it took the entire session to unfold, not a sharp flush. That's the brake effect showing up in the price action itself: with the regime shift below Total Gamma Flip sustaining pressure and no single dominant wall to produce one clean break, the decline ground out slowly across the full session rather than resolving quickly in either direction.

VXN confirmed the same story. VXN opened/anchored near its own baseline (~22.33) and, notably, could not sustain a drop below that baseline through the New York AM session despite the price decline already underway — the inverse-confirmation pattern the framework expects when a decline carries real conviction rather than being a temporary flush (VXN staying elevated / unable to fall as price falls signals sustained fear). From there VXN pushed up toward +1SD/+2SD (22.51–22.70), oscillating in that zone through New York PM — consistent with the sustained sell-side pressure that kept price pinned well below the overnight highs for the rest of the session.
