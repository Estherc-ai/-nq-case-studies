Methodology Correction — Asian SD Bands Need the Same Skew-Regime Rule as VXN

The pre-market 0DTE σ (0.52) used above was captured before the open, while skew was still elevated (SKEW 0.212 pre-market). Once the market opened and repositioned, 0DTE σ settled to 0.35 by 9:45 AM — a meaningful repricing, the same pattern the framework's skew-regime rule already accounts for on the VXN side (elevated pre-market skew → wait for post-open repositioning). That rule evidently needs to extend to the Asian SD band input too, not just the VXN matrix.

Recalculated with the post-open σ (0.35):

σ_daily = 0.35 ÷ 15.8745 = 0.022049
SD_move = 28,614 × 0.022049 = 631.03
−1SD = 28,614 − 631.03 = 27,982.97

This corrected −1SD (27,982.97) lands almost exactly on the 98DTE Put Wall (28,000) — a genuine cross-tool confluence between the SD band and OI wall structure that the stale pre-market band (27,676.54) completely missed. Price actually touched 27,982 intraday — an essentially exact hit on the corrected band, not just a nearby approximation — before continuing to the true session low of 27,940, which itself landed between the corrected SD lower and the 0DTE Put Wall (27,825). That's a real-time exact-level confirmation followed by the final low bracketed by two independently-sourced structural levels, not a retrospective coincidence.

Revised procedure for future sessions: if pre-market 0DTE skew reads elevated (as it did here, SKEW 0.212), don't lock in the Asian SD band from the pre-market read. Wait for post-open repositioning, recalculate the band with the updated σ, and cross-check the result against the nearest Put Wall / Call Wall. Alignment between the corrected SD band and an OI wall is a stronger, double-confirmed read of where the session's structural floor actually sits than either tool alone — and a more reliable profit-taking/bottom-identification signal than trusting the pre-market number in isolation.

04 — Session Mechanism: Flush Through a Pre-Identified Unhedged Zone, Live Wall Broken to Confirm the Trend

RTH opened at 28,591 — just below the Asian baseline (28,614) and +0.761% above ZG (28,375), a Condition 7 open. Price ticked up immediately to touch the Asian baseline, then reversed hard. Straight after the open, NQ flushed continuously downward, breaking the 98DTE Call Wall (28,550) without pause, and dropping directly into the pre-identified unhedged zone between ZG (28,375) and 28,429 — a thin patch in dealer hedging structure that had been flagged from the pre-market chart itself, not discovered after the fact. Price moved through that zone with visibly less resistance than the levels above and below it, consistent with the pre-market read.

At 9:48 AM, a large live call wall suddenly formed at 28,429 — a genuine, sizeable concentration, not a thin one. Price broke straight down through it anyway. A big wall forming and immediately failing to hold is a meaningful signal in its own right: rather than reading as a defended level, it read as confirmation that the downtrend was continuing — dealers building a wall that gets swept through tells you the prevailing flow was strong enough to run through freshly-built defense, not just an already-thin pre-market gap.

Price continued straight through the 98DTE Put Wall (28,000) and the 0DTE Put Wall (27,825), bottoming at a session low of 27,940, before consolidating near the Put Wall zone for the remainder of the session into the close.

This was a Condition 7 → 3 mutation in the most literal sense — the session opened on the positive side of ZG, immediately crossed back through it, and spent the entire rest of the day in deep negative-gamma territory near Put Wall, never attempting to reclaim ZG.
