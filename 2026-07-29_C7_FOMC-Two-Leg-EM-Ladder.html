04 — Session Mechanism: Two Flushes, Two Expected-Move Horizons

RTH opened at 27,917, +0.493% above ZG (27,780) — a Condition 7 open. The first 15-minute candle immediately tested the Major/Gamma Wall (28,000), rejecting there. From that rejection, price broke down and ran its first leg lower, bottoming at 27,411 around 12:15 PM ET — landing almost exactly on two independently-sourced levels at once: the Asian −1SD band (27,398.10) and the IV 2-Day Expected Move (27,390). A confluence between a statistical price-distance measure and an options-implied-move measure, at the same level, at the same time.

Chart 1 — MNQU26, Full Session View: open 27,917, Call Wall reject, first flush to 27,411 (12:15 PM) at the Asian SD/2-Day EM confluence, reversal to session high 28,100, second flush through ZG at 2:00 PM (FOMC statement), session low ~27,205 at the 3-Day EM, recovery into close 27,617.25

Price then reversed sharply, running all the way back up to the session high of 28,100, just above the secondary Call Wall (28,090) — a full round trip from the first low back into the pre-market resistance cluster, well ahead of the FOMC statement.

From there, the second and deeper leg began. Price declined back through ZG (27,780) right around 2:00 PM ET — the exact moment the FOMC statement was released — and continued lower through the afternoon, ultimately bottoming at the true session low of ~27,205 around 4:00 PM ET. This second low landed almost exactly on the IV 3-Day Expected Move (27,240) — a deeper, longer-horizon level than either tool that caught the first leg, consistent with a scheduled macro catalyst justifying the market pricing in a larger possible move than a normal session would need. Price then recovered into the close, finishing at 27,617.25 — just 17 points above Put Wall (27,600) and comfortably above the 3-Day EM.

This is a clean demonstration of the Expected Move ladder scaling with move severity: the smaller, earlier dip (12:15 PM, pre-news) was caught by the 1–2 Day horizon; the larger, news-driven flush (post-2:00 PM) was caught by the 3-Day horizon. Neither the Asian SD band nor a single EM horizon alone would have called both extremes — it took reading the full ladder.

04a — VXN: Anticipatory Elevation Ahead of a Known Catalyst

VXN's behavior this session was distinct from every prior write-up this week, and worth naming as its own pattern. After an early spike toward +3SD (29.83) during New York AM, VXN did not decay back down the way it has on calmer sessions — it stayed above +3SD from roughly 10:00 AM through 1:00 PM ET, a full three-hour stretch of sustained elevation rather than a spike-and-fade, well before the FOMC statement even landed. This is a materially different pattern from every prior session this week, where VXN typically touched an extreme band and reversed within a shorter window. Holding above +3SD for three hours straight is consistent with the market pricing in known, scheduled uncertainty ahead of time — dealers and traders holding elevated hedges through the morning specifically because they knew a major catalyst was coming at 2:00 PM, not because of any single triggering price move.

Chart 2 — VXN, Full Session View: early spike toward +3SD and unusually sustained elevation through New York AM/early afternoon, brief crush toward −2SD around the 2:00–2:30 PM statement/press-conference window, then a sharp spike through +3SD to ~30.84 as price made its final decline into the 4:00 PM low

Around 2:00–2:30 PM ET — the statement release and the start of the press conference — VXN dipped sharply toward −2SD (27.68), a brief relief/vol-crush reaction consistent with the headline itself being in line with expectations (a hold, as anticipated). But that calm didn't last: VXN then reversed hard and spiked straight through +3SD, closing near 30.84 — well outside the pre-market matrix's own +3σ level (29.83) — as the press conference commentary (a divided 9–3 vote, hawkish rhetoric on inflation) reignited uncertainty. This spike coincided with price's continued decline into the 4:00 PM low, before both price and VXN stabilized into the close.

The takeaway worth carrying into future FOMC-day sessions: VXN's pre-catalyst behavior on a known-event day looks structurally different from a normal session — sustained elevation rather than spike-and-revert — and the vol reaction to the event itself can be genuinely two-phase (initial crush on the headline, followed by a second move on the press conference/details), rather than a single spike at the announcement time.

Skew's pre-FOMC behavior tells the same story from a third angle, and it's a genuinely different shape from anything logged this week:

Rather than the whipsaw seen on Jul 28, this is a clean, steady, one-directional compression — SKEW fell almost monotonically from 0.187 to essentially flat (0.010) over 90 minutes. What makes this notable is what IV was doing at the same time: IV was rising (0.55 → 0.61) while SKEW compressed toward zero. That combination has a specific meaning: overall expected movement was increasing (consistent with the approaching 2:00 PM catalyst), but that increase wasn't asymmetrically loaded toward the downside — puts and calls were becoming more expensive together, not puts pulling further ahead of calls. In plain terms: the market was pricing in a genuinely large, two-sided possible move, not a one-sided fear trade. That's a meaningfully different setup from every prior compression event logged this week, which occurred after a move (confirming exhaustion) rather than before one (confirming genuine two-way uncertainty ahead of a scheduled binary-ish event).

This is consistent with — and helps explain — why the first flush (to 27,411 at 12:15 PM) happened before skew had fully flattened, and why the second, larger flush came well after this compression had already resolved: the compression here wasn't marking the exhaustion of a specific move the way it did on Jul 24/27/28, it was marking the market settling into "big move coming, direction unknown" ahead of the actual news.
