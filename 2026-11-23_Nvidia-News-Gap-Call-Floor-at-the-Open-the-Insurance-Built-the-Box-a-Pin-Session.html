1 — Market Context

Before the open on Monday, Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, 11 days after his resignation. Nvidia closed +31.48% at $165.05, adding about $950 billion in market value, the largest one-day gain in market value on record for any company. The Nasdaq had its biggest opening gain on record; the Nasdaq Composite closed +6.51% (+1,471.83) at 24,089.56, its best day since April 9, 2025 (the 23rd-largest daily rise since 1971) and the Nasdaq-100 +6.23%. It followed a correction week, at the start of the Thanksgiving-shortened week. The US flash PMIs were released at 9:45 ET.

NQ opened at 26,884 and closed at 26,800: +1,556 (+6.16%) on Friday's close (25,244). Both extremes came in the opening bars — the high, 27,149, first, then the low, 26,452 — and the rest of the session stayed inside 26,720 ↔ 26,940.

Two things decided the day: the news moved price past the whole pre-market map before the open, and the protection bought to insure that gain built an Inverted Range that held price for the rest of the session.

The day in brief. 🏷️ Day type: Call Chase (pre-market, 8:45 news) → Call Floor · Hedged (9:30) → Inverted Range (11:45).

A Call Chase is a day when the largest call sits above price and pulls it up. A Call Floor is a day when price sits above all the big calls: they act as floors below it. An Inverted Range is a box with the largest put above price (the lid) and the largest call below (the floor). Hedged means protection (puts bought as insurance, SKEW ≥ 0.10) is held. Cashed means that protection was sold; released means a strike has fallen to half of its own peak or less. A put stack is a group of nearby puts whose sizes add up.

  • 🟨 8:45: the news spike. The Nvidia headline lifted price from the 25,845 pre-market Call Wall / largest call (with ZG at 25,820) to near 26,720 before the open — about 1,000 points past the pre-market map.
  • 🟨 9:30: the open sweeps both ways. High 27,149 (29 through the top of a put stack at 27,120), then low 26,452.
  • 🟨 9:46: Call Floor · Hedged. Price sat above all the big pre-market calls; the open print's largest call, 26,940, was tagged at the open. SKEW 0.038 → 0.130: protection bought on the spike, while the book stayed call-side (+2.34B). Insurance on the gain, not a bet on a drop.
  • 🟨 10:00: the cap is born. A new call at 27,070 (+160M) and a put pair at 27,110–27,120 (−120 / −115M) appeared above.
  • 🟨 10:30: the climax under the cap. The book jumped to +6.25B, 27,070 grew to +540M at price, protection was added (0.163), and the strikes beyond were thin.
  • 🟨 11:45 → 13:45: the box is built. Each pullback added puts above price — 26,870 grew from −235M to −410M — while the call floor 26,720–26,730 grew to +485M. At 13:45 the largest put sat above and the largest call below: an Inverted Range.
  • The calls above never outgrew 26,940 (they matched it once, on the 10:30 climax print). With no new destination, price returned to 26,940 and pinned.
  • VXN opened at its anchor (45.25), fell below −3σ in the first minute and stayed there all session. Close 26,800, inside the box.

2 — Day Focus: the 8:45 News, the Call Floor, and the Box That Insurance Built

  1. 8:45: the news moved price past the map. The pre-market destination, the 25,845 call, was reached and passed before the open. The open print built a new map near the new price.
  2. The open: Call Floor · Hedged. Price was above all the big pre-market calls. Protection was bought on the spike (SKEW 0.038 → 0.130) while the book stayed call-heavy: holders kept their calls and insured the gain. With protection held, a Call Floor pulls back before it trends.
  3. The pullback took the shape of an Inverted Range. The protection is puts, and puts bought above price become a lid. Price was held between that lid and the call floor below while the market digested the move.
